# 7. Payments are one of the most important architectural decisions

This is where I would not build our own wallet/escrow system.

You are essentially creating:

```text
Student
   │
   │ Payment
   ▼
Platform
   │
   ├── Platform commission
   │
   ▼
Mentor
```

For India, use a marketplace-capable payment provider rather than attempting to hold and redistribute customer money ourselves.

For example, Cashfree's current Easy Split product supports marketplace vendor onboarding, payment splitting, settlements, refunds and reconciliation. (https://www.cashfree.com/docs/api-reference/payments/latest/split/easy-split-overview?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Razorpay Route similarly supports marketplace linked accounts, payment transfers and configurable settlement flows. (https://razorpay.com/route/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The exact provider should be selected based on:

```text
India payments
International cards
International payouts
UPI
Multi-currency
Refunds
Chargebacks
KYC
Marketplace settlement
Webhook quality
Tax reporting
Supported countries
Fees
```

This also needs legal/accounting review because an online marketplace that collects consideration for third-party suppliers can have GST and e-commerce-operator implications in India. CBIC specifically identifies electronic platforms facilitating electronic commerce and describes TCS obligations in applicable marketplace situations. (https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/sectoral-faq.html?ld=SDINSOADirect&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

So we need to design the payment subsystem before launch, not bolt payments on afterward.

# 14. India's current privacy situation matters

Because you're starting in India, we should design around the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the 2025 Rules rather than treating privacy as a generic checkbox.

The Rules were notified in November 2025, and the Act defines a child as someone who has not completed 18 years of age. (https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa?pageTitle=Digit&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This is particularly important for your product because “student” does not necessarily mean adult.

For an initial commercial launch, I would seriously consider:

```text
Initial platform:
18+ only
```

That dramatically simplifies the first version of the safety/privacy model.

If we later support under-18 students, we should build an explicit child/guardian framework rather than merely adding an age checkbox. The notified rules include requirements around verifiable parental consent. (https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## The direction I'd take

My strongest recommendation is:

Next.js + TypeScript + NestJS + PostgreSQL + Redis + AWS + marketplace payment provider + strict RBAC + event/audit architecture + ISO/SOC control mapping from day one.

And I would not make microservices, AI matching, social feeds, or fancy analytics the first priority.

The difficult parts of this company are actually:

identity → verification → matching → contracts → payments → disputes → reputation → privacy → security → auditability.

Get those right and the social/network features become much easier to layer on.

The Indian privacy regime is already sufficiently mature that we should account for DPDP from the architecture stage, and the marketplace payment model needs to be designed with the applicable RBI/payment-provider and GST structure rather than treating the platform as a normal SaaS checkout.
